# FinBridge Copilot Prompting Starter Guide

A practical guide for getting useful help from Microsoft 365 Copilot in your everyday work. You do not need special wording. Start with what you are working on, say what you want Copilot to do, and describe the result you need.

## The simple prompt formula

**Task + context + format**

For example, open the email, document, or spreadsheet you want help with, then type:

> Summarise this email for a busy manager in five bullet points. Include the decisions and actions.

You can make the answer better by adding a time period, audience, length, tone, or specific points to include.

## Outlook examples

### 1. Catch up on an email conversation

> Summarise this email conversation in five bullet points. Show the decisions, questions that still need an answer, actions, who will do each action, and any deadline.

This is useful when a long conversation has built up and you need the current position quickly.

### 2. Prepare a reply

> Draft a polite reply to this email confirming that I will provide the requested figures by Friday. Keep it under 120 words, use a professional but friendly tone, and do not invent any figures or promises.

Read the draft carefully before sending it. Check names, dates, figures, and the intended recipients.

### 3. Prepare for a meeting

> Use my emails about this week's finance review to create a short meeting brief. Include the purpose of the meeting, decisions needed, risks, and questions I should ask. Use headings and keep it to one page.

## Word examples

### 1. Improve a first draft

> Rewrite this update for senior managers. Keep the meaning and all figures unchanged. Make it clearer, remove repetition, and use short paragraphs with headings.

Copilot can improve structure and readability, but you remain responsible for checking that the message still says what you mean.

### 2. Summarise a document

> Summarise this report in 150 words for a colleague who has not read it. Include the main message, three important facts, decisions required, and any stated risks. Do not add information that is not in the report.

### 3. Turn notes into an action list

> Turn these meeting notes into a table with four columns: what needs doing, who will do it, due date, and progress. Write “To confirm” when the notes do not say who will do something or when it is due. Put urgent actions first.

## Excel examples

### 1. Explain a change in the figures

> Review the figures in this spreadsheet and find the three biggest changes from one month to the next. Show the item, the old figure, the new figure, and the percentage change. Then explain what changed in plain English. Do not change my figures.

Check the calculations and source values before using the result in a report.

### 2. Find and explain a trend

> Look at this table for this financial year. Tell me what is steadily going up or down, which figures look unusual, and whether anything is missing. Put the findings in a short table and explain them in plain English.

### 3. Help prepare a management view

> Create a suggested summary table from this spreadsheet showing total income, total costs, and the difference for each month. Keep the original figures unchanged and show how each total was worked out.

Review the suggested calculations before sharing them. Copilot can help you explore a spreadsheet, but it does not replace financial review or approval.

## A few habits that make prompts work better

- Say who the answer is for: “for a senior manager” or “for a new colleague.”
- Set a useful shape: “five bullet points,” “a table,” or “under 150 words.”
- Say exactly what to include: “include decisions, who needs to act, deadlines, and risks.”
- Set boundaries: “use only the information provided” and “do not change the original data.”
- Ask a follow-up: “What information is missing?” or “Show me where this came from.”
- Check the result. Copilot can misunderstand context or produce an incorrect summary, calculation, or draft.

## Keep information safe

Use the Day 1 data-safety habit: **do not paste real confidential information, personal data, passwords, or credentials into a public AI tool.** This includes client details, payroll information, board or M&A documents, account numbers, and identifying email content. You can use FinBridge Microsoft 365 Copilot for approved work, while still following the access and handling rules for the information you are using.

Microsoft 365 Copilot is approved for FinBridge work within the organisation's controls. Before using a public AI website or app, remove names, email addresses, client details, account numbers, and other identifying information. Replace them with simple labels such as “Client A” or “Manager 1.” In Copilot, follow the sharing and protection instructions shown for the file. If you are unsure whether something is sensitive, stop and ask the service desk or your manager before sharing it.

Finally, review every Copilot answer before you send it, publish it, or use it in a financial decision.
